# Nassem – My KPI and Chart

I chose the first and second key performance indicators that were visually constructed using a histogram in order to convert them into a graph using lines, especially since data where time is an important factor is best represented in the form of lines. We see this often in the prices of gold, oil, and even digital currencies like Bitcoin, which are constructed using graph lines.  

Unlike a histogram which groups values into buckets and loses time order, a line chart keeps months in sequence so the reader can immediately see that the two big spikes happened recently, not randomly throughout the period. The colored area under the MoM panel makes it easy to spot at a glance whether a month was gaining or losing momentum without reading any numbers.  


# Marwan – KPI and Chart

## Why did you pick different KPIs? What made yours feel important?

In the first chart, the focus was primarily on the **Revenue Trend** as a single continuous flow. In the optimized version, we introduced additional KPIs: the **Average (Avg)**, **Peak**, and **Low**.  

**Why it matters:**  
These KPIs transform the chart from a simple line into a comparative tool. Without the Average line, it's difficult to judge if a specific month's performance is actually successful or just baseline. Highlighting the Peak and Low provides immediate reference points, allowing the viewer to quickly identify the best and worst performing periods without scanning the entire axis.  


Furthermore, I changed how revenue is visualized by adding **Individual Bars** for each month. This makes each month act as its own standalone KPI. While the line shows movement, the bars allow the viewer to perceive the **Absolute Magnitude** of each month's performance. This provides a "physical volume" to the data, making it much easier to compare the weight of one month against another compared to a line chart alone.
